# Neuromodulation as control of learning: literature notes

Thread: ACh/NE as expected vs unexpected uncertainty, LC network reset, human learning-rate adaptation, pupil-linked arousal, dopamine RPE / distributional RL and challenges, and AI implementations. Written 2026-11-04.

## Summary

1. Biology. It is well supported that human learning rate rises after changepoints and tracks a normative-like adaptive rule (Nassar 2010: 30 subjects; Piray & Daw 2020: 40/44 and 64% of participants in two datasets). The most decisive human evidence that surprise should be interpreted by context is Nassar, Bruckner & Frank 2019: in 39 subjects, surprise raised learning in a changepoint context and lowered it in an oddball context (surprise x condition beta = 0.71, t = 9.9). Separating changepoint probability from relative uncertainty is weaker: the two are collinear by construction, the reduced model was "quantitatively inconsistent" with subjects in the original paper, and a differently structured model (volatile Kalman filter) fits better in model comparison. The NE=unexpected / ACh=expected mapping is contested in pharmacology (Marshall 2016: NE effect did not survive correction, ACh antagonist changed both learning rates). Pupil as an LC readout is contested (Megemont 2022). Distributional RL in dopamine has direct but small-N data; RPE exclusivity is actively disputed.
2. AI. Nothing I opened shows a neuromodulation-inspired mechanism beating a well-tuned Adam/AdamW baseline with a tuned schedule on a standard benchmark. Positive results are against weak or non-matching baselines (gradient-only ablation, plasticity-preservation baselines, untuned large LSTM) or are about robustness to initial LR rather than final performance. Independent audits show that claimed optimizer speedups mostly collapse under fair tuning (Wen et al.: claimed 1.4-2x shrinks to at most 1.4x, 1.1x at 1.2B params; tuning only the AdamW learning rate gave up to 2x at 100M). Learning-rate-free methods were "not clearly better" than calibrated AdamW/NadamW on AlgoPerf.
3. Dropped property. Best-supported: AI "surprise" gates map surprise monotonically to more plasticity. The biological/normative computation infers WHY the outcome was surprising (new regime vs outlier vs noise) and the sign of the learning-rate effect flips accordingly. Second, as a hypothesis with partial support: one inferred latent coordinates several plasticity processes (rate, reset, consolidation) together; AI uses separate heuristic gates. "Global shared scalar" is itself contested in biology (LC modularity, heterogeneous dopamine responses) and the AI evidence on granularity is mixed. Details in the Dropped properties section.
4. Three Colab experiments are specified below, each with a tuned-constant-LR baseline, a normative or oracle ceiling, and a falsifying result.

## AI implementations and effect sizes

All entries below were opened via alphaXiv text unless stated.

### Neuromodulated plasticity (Miconi: Backpropamine) https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10585
- Mechanism: a network-computed scalar M(t) replaces the plasticity rate eta in Hebbian updates (simple), or gates an eligibility trace (retroactive).
- Penn Treebank, small LSTM (4.8M params, parameter-matched, "equally-powered grid-search", 16 runs, mean +/- 95% CI): baseline 104.26 +/- 0.22; differentiable plasticity 103.80 +/- 0.25; simple neuromodulation 102.65 +/- 0.30; retroactive 102.48 +/- 0.28. Simple vs retroactive: p = 0.066 (not significant).
- Large AWD-LSTM (24.2M params, 5 runs, median (min, max)): baseline 62.48 (62.40, 62.60) vs neuromodulated 61.44 (61.37, 61.68). No hyperparameter tuning of the plastic model.
- Baselines are LSTMs, not tuned modern optimizers or architectures. The paper says its numbers are "far from state-of-the-art".
- Granularity finding (appendix A.1.2): variants with one modulatory neuron per node or one for the whole network "performed worse" in preliminary experiments and were not evaluated; the paper used one modulator per LSTM layer. The large model uses per-neuron multiples of a common modulator.

### NeuMoSync (TMLR 06/2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04358
- Mechanism: a transformer controller emits per-neuron coefficients (synaptic modulation, consolidation gating, activation linearity, offset), conditioned on current input and network state; trained end-to-end by gradient. Not a scalar.
- Baselines (CBP, ReDo, CReLU, L2Init, EWC, LayerNorm, Hare & Tortoise, etc.) were grid-searched, 3 seeds. Table 1 rows I could read (CBP, CReLU, ReDo, L2Init+EWC only; other rows not captured): final accuracy, Random Label CIFAR-10: NeuMoSync 64.74 (1.96) vs CBP 38.21 (0.24), CReLU 35.03 (0.25), ReDo 27.14 (0.87). Shuffle CIFAR-10: 92.84 (0.36) vs CBP 90.14 (0.47), CReLU 89.41 (0.34). Class Split CIFAR-100: 78.68 (0.91) vs CBP 72.36 (0.67). Class Split T-ImageNet: 86.37 (1.06) vs CReLU 84.27 (1.02).
- Gain is not faster intrinsic learning: LCA_0 for NeuMoSync (e.g. 10.68 on Random Label CIFAR-10) is lower than several baselines; the paper attributes gains to knowledge transfer.
- Ablation: removing the controller and learning the same coefficients per neuron collapses performance; but the controller sees the input, and removing input conditioning also hurts (stronger on memorisation). One caveat I found: the paper's own Appendix G.4 replaced per-neuron plasticity signals with one network-wide pair of signals and performance "decreases"; on Shuffle CIFAR-10 "a noticeable drop", not a collapse. So coordination helps, but a single global scalar was worse than fine-grained signals in this architecture.
- Not compared against a tuned LR schedule or Adam-with-schedule on a final-accuracy benchmark; comparisons are to plasticity-preservation methods on plasticity benchmarks. Controller is 5-8% of parameters for MLP/CNN.

### NeuroPlastic optimizer (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26297
- Mechanism: per-parameter multiplicative factor from normalised gradient magnitude, EMA of gradient magnitude, and an Adam-style m/sqrt(v) ratio, then clipped and RMS-stabilised. No hazard or changepoint inference.
- Results are against a "gradient-only ablation" that itself keeps clipped gradient-magnitude modulation (not plain Adam): MNIST +0.001 absolute, Fashion-MNIST about +0.006, 3 seeds, 10 epochs, lr 1e-3. On CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 the paper states it "does not surpass well-tuned standard optimizers", and in the MNIST-tuned transfer the gradient-only ablation was slightly higher (about 0.001). Many naive variants underperform standard optimizers.
- Verdict from the paper's own numbers: no evidence of beating Adam/AdamW.

### Soft Reset, Galashov et al. (NeurIPS 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04034
- Mechanism: learned drift gamma_t per layer (weights and biases separately). Gamma < 1 pulls parameters toward initialisation and raises the learning rate in the same step (eq. 17-18). This is a learned coupling of two plasticity processes (reset and LR) through one variable.
- Estimated online by gradient on the next batch's likelihood. Gamma drops at task boundaries without being told them.
- Non-Bayesian per-parameter gamma "did not perform well"; per-layer shared gamma worked better there; the Bayesian per-parameter version was best overall but costly. Performance advantage over online SGD grows with task duration (infrequent change). Drift-model learning rate is the most sensitive hyperparameter; L2-Init and Shrink&Perturb each had essentially one good value.
- I did not capture headline accuracy numbers (reported in figures). A "perfect soft reset" oracle with known boundaries exists (appendix H.1), which makes it a usable ceiling.

### Surprise-gated memory and test-time learning
- Titans (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663): surprise = gradient of the associative-memory loss, with momentum ("past surprise") and data-dependent decay eta_t and step theta_t; equivalent to SGD with momentum and weight decay on a meta-network. Training of the whole model uses AdamW lr 4e-4, cosine schedule. Ablation ranking by contribution: weight decay (forgetting) > momentum > convolution > persistent memory. The surprise gating is not isolated from "a delta rule with decay"; I did not capture the benchmark table numbers. Baselines for 400M come from other papers' reported numbers.
- SGM, independent research note (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31495): frozen backbone with a prediction-error gate for writes to a non-parametric store. On a 1,000-class stream, surprise-gated writes (84.2) matched full replay (83.7) at half the storage; the paper itself says this is "within single-run variation" and that the gate bought compression, not accuracy. Single-seed system 1.
- "Surprise Alone Learns Lies" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39840): surprise-driven selection absorbed 84-93% of all streamed lies (99-100% for known-fact lies) in LoRA students (1.5B-3B). Against random selection with the same budget: lies per 1,000 learned claims 254 vs 257 (paired difference -3, 95% CI -15 to +9); for known-fact lies surprise absorbed 99.5% vs 88.9% (+11 points, CI +9 to +13). A claim that contradicts what the model knows is surprising by construction.

### Normative comparators in AI
- Bayes Factor Surprise (Liakoni et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02936) shows Adams & MacKay's message-passing algorithm has the same surprise modulation and is exact but O(t) in cost; approximations with finite messages (MP_N, particle filters, VarSMiLe) are derived. I did not capture its benchmark numbers. I did not open Adams & MacKay 2007 itself.
- Piray & Daw VKF above is a cheap alternative comparator.

### What strong baselines look like (why the numbers above matter)
- Wen et al., "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046): claimed speedups 1.4-2x; after coordinate-descent tuning of every optimizer across 0.1B-1.2B params, speedups never exceeded 1.4x (Fig. 3) and fall to 1.1x at 1.2B at 8x Chinchilla. Tuning only the learning rate of the standard GPT-3 AdamW recipe gave up to 2x at 100M. Optimizer rankings can flip during LR decay, so intermediate checkpoints mislead.
- AlgoPerf v1 self-tuning (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13036): Schedule-Free AdamW v2 (PyTorch) first with score 0.5679; an AdEMAMix submission that degenerated to AdamW second at 0.5191; learning rate and momentum are the most sensitive hyperparameters; top-5% LR regions can be nearly or entirely disjoint across workloads.
- Kasimbeg et al., hyperparameter-free (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24005): literature-default LR-free methods (D-Adapt, Mechanic, Prodigy, DoG, MoMo, COCOB) did poorly; calibrated Prodigy and Mechanic ranked 3rd/4th, "competitive with (but not clearly better than)" calibrated AdamW/NadamW. Best LR-free methods still needed a warmup and decay schedule.
- GALA (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08419): online gradient-alignment LR adaptation on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10/100, Flowers102), 3 seeds; robust across initial LRs and "competitive" with the best runs of SGD and Adam. No final-accuracy gain over tuned baselines is claimed.
- Not read beyond a title/abstract snippet in search results: Celo2, other learned-optimizer papers, SOLAR, LionVote. I make no claim about them.

### Claims that did not hold up (from the sources above)
- 2x-class optimizer speedups over AdamW: mostly baseline under-tuning (Wen et al.).
- Surprise gating as a better selection rule: no better than random on poisoned streams (Surprise Alone Learns Lies); no accuracy gain over full replay (SGM).
- LR-free adaptivity removing tuning: no detectable advantage after calibration (Kasimbeg et al.).
- Plasticity-modulated optimizer vs standard optimizers on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10: not better (NeuroPlastic's own results).
- Globally shared modulator: single network-wide modulator was worse than per-layer in Backpropamine's preliminary experiments, and global-vs-per-neuron signals were worse in NeuMoSync's ablation.

## Dropped load-bearing properties

### 1. Interpretation of surprise by inferred cause, with sign flip (best supported)
Evidence that biology does this: Nassar 2019 (n = 39) found surprise increased learning rate in the changepoint context and decreased it in the oddball context (interaction beta = 0.71, t = 9.9), with the matching normative forms alpha = tau + Omega - tau*Omega (changepoint) vs alpha = tau - Omega*tau (oddball). Liakoni et al. derive the same point independently: with an outlier generative model "surprise should attenuate learning, rather than accelerate it". The evidence that this is neuromodulatory specifically is weak: the human signal was P300 (EEG), and the P300-LC link is not tested in what I read.

Evidence that AI drops it: gradient-magnitude or NLL gates (NeuroPlastic, Titans' momentary surprise, surprise-selected replay) are monotone in surprise by construction. "Surprise Alone Learns Lies" is a direct failure case: a lie about a known fact is the most surprising input, and the surprise gate absorbed 99.5% of such lies vs 88.9% for random selection. Titans' data-dependent eta_t could in principle learn to ignore a surprise, but the paper does not test an outlier-vs-regime-change stream.

A closely related dropped term: relative (reducible) uncertainty tau, which is separate from changepoint probability in the Nassar model. Gradient-statistic gates have no analogue of tau, and Piray & Daw list fixed observation noise as an open limitation even in the normative literature.

### 2. One inferred latent coordinating several plasticity processes (hypothesis, partial support)
The conjecture is that the biological control signal is shared across processes (belief update rate, network/state reset, consolidation, attention gain) rather than being a per-optimizer learning rate. What I verified:
- For: Bouret & Sara's reset proposal (abstract level) says phasic LC activity reorganises networks, i.e. a state reset, not only a rate change. Marshall 2016: a single drug (biperiden) shifted both learning-rate parameters and three response-sensitivity parameters, though systemic drugs cannot show that one endogenous signal does this. Soft Reset (AI) already couples reset and LR through one gamma and found it works, with a known-boundary oracle that does better than hard reset.
- Against or unresolved: LC modularity (snippet-level; Foote & Berridge dissent in the same snippet); dopamine responses in Dabney 2020 are heterogeneous across neurons (a vector, not a scalar); NeuMoSync's own limitation text says neuromodulatory effects are not uniform at the level of individual neurons and notes that its model uses fine-grained per-neuron control, unlike biology's population-level targeting. AI granularity evidence is mixed: Backpropamine found single-network-wide modulators worse than per-layer; Soft Reset found per-layer sharing helped the non-Bayesian version; NeuMoSync found global scalars worse than per-neuron signals but coordination via a shared controller better than independent per-neuron parameters.
- No paper I opened tests one inferred hazard latent driving LR, reset and consolidation together against separate gates. That is the gap Experiment 2 targets.

### 3. Minor
- State dependence / inverted U: LR effect of an arousal manipulation was positive at small baseline pupil (0.113) and absent at large (-0.037) in Nassar 2012. AI modulators are monotone with no saturation.
- Subjects under-react to changepoints relative to the reduced Bayesian model (Nassar 2010); AI gates are tuned to maximise reactivity.

## Candidate experiments

Common rules for all three: constant-LR Adam (and SGD) baselines get the same tuning budget as every proposed method (the Wen et al. result that LR tuning alone can give up to 2x at 100M is the reason). Report seeds >= 10 for Tier A, >= 5 for Tier B, with bootstrap CIs. Metric of record is "fraction of the gap closed": (E_tuned_constant - E_method) / (E_tuned_constant - E_ceiling). Pre-register thresholds before running; the numbers below are proposals, not predictions from the literature. Each condition is designed to finish in under 10-15 minutes on a free Colab runtime; nothing here has been run.

### Experiment 1. Does the sign flip matter? (changepoint vs outlier vs noise streams)
- Question: does inferring cause (regime change vs outlier) beat a monotone surprise gate for gradient learners?
- Tier A (CPU, seconds): 1-D mean-tracking stream, Gaussian noise, hazard H, with three contexts as in Nassar 2019: changepoint, oddball (about 14% uniform outliers, no mean change), and a mixed stream. Learners: fixed-alpha delta rule (alpha grid), monotone surprise gate alpha = f(|error|/sigma) tuned, full BOCPD-style run-length posterior with change-vs-outlier hypotheses (the Nassar 2019 forms), oracle that knows true changepoints.
- Tier B (Colab T4 or CPU, a few minutes per run): online two-layer MLP regressing a piecewise-stationary teacher; same three contexts, with outliers injected in labels. Learners: Adam constant LR, tuned over a grid; Adam with a monotone loss/grad-norm-spike gate on LR (the NeuroPlastic/Titans-style mapping); Adam with LR scaled by a changepoint posterior from a BOCPD run on the per-sample loss stream, with the outlier hypothesis; oracle LR boost at true changepoints and suppression at true outliers.
- Normative ceiling: Tier A full Bayesian posterior (Adams & MacKay / Liakoni form) is the ceiling for the linear-Gaussian case; Tier B ceiling is the oracle-with-known-events learner, not a proof of optimality.
- Falsifiers (any one kills the idea as stated): (a) on the oddball and mixed streams the context-aware gate closes no more of the gap than the monotone gate (difference in gap-closed with CI containing 0, or < 0.05); (b) no gated method beats tuned constant-LR Adam by more than seed noise in Tier B; (c) in Tier B the BOCPD signal computed from loss fails to separate injected outliers from changes (AUC < 0.6), which would mean the inference cannot be made from the signals an optimizer sees.

### Experiment 2. One latent, many knobs (shared hazard signal vs separate gates)
- Question: does a single inferred change signal that simultaneously raises LR, shrinks toward initialisation, and resets Adam moment estimates beat the same mechanisms gated independently?
- Setup: random-label MNIST and Permuted MNIST, small MLP, 30-50 tasks, a few epochs per task (the benchmark family used by Galashov et al. and NeuMoSync; both report on it). Metric: average online task accuracy across tasks.
- Conditions with matched hyperparameter-search budgets: (A) Adam tuned constant LR; (B) Adam plus tuned Shrink-and-Perturb or L2-Init (published as having a narrow good setting); (C) Soft Reset per-layer gamma (closest published coupling of reset and LR); (D) shared-signal: one BOCPD or gamma-style posterior h_t drives LR boost, shrink, and moment reset together; (E) separate gates: LR gate, shrink gate, moment reset each driven by its own heuristic signal (loss spike, dormant-unit fraction, gradient norm), each tuned; (F) shared signal driving only LR (isolates the value of coupling).
- Ceiling: known-task-boundary oracle that applies the same three actions at true boundaries (analogous to the "perfect soft reset" in Galashov et al., appendix H.1). Also a stationary control stream (no task changes) where the gate must stay quiet; report accuracy cost on it.
- Falsifier: D is not better than max(B, C, E) by more than one pooled standard error across >= 5 seeds, or D is worse than F (coupling more knobs hurts), or D loses more than 1 accuracy point to A on the stationary control. Any of these means shared coordination is not load-bearing here.

### Experiment 3. Stochasticity vs volatility (the ACh/NE-style decomposition in a gradient learner)
- Question: does separating expected uncertainty (noise) from unexpected change help, and do surprise-based gates fail when noise rises without a change?
- Stream: same piecewise-stationary regression as Experiment 1 but with switches in label-noise variance and, separately, in the teacher. Four event types: mean change only, noise increase only, both, neither.
- Learners: constant-LR Adam (tuned); monotone surprise gate; two-signal gate with a running noise estimate that discounts errors (alpha = tau + (1 - tau)*Omega form, with Omega computed against estimated noise); Tier A normative ceiling: the Kalman filter with learned volatility (Piray & Daw's VKF, which treats observation noise as a free parameter) or the exact Bayesian model with known noise.
- Falsifier: on noise-increase-only events, the monotone gate raises the LR and loses accuracy relative to constant-LR Adam (this is the predicted failure, and its absence would show the failure mode is not real for gradient learners), AND the two-signal gate recovers it. If the monotone gate does not hurt, or the two-signal gate does not help (gap-closed difference < 0.05 with CI containing 0), the decomposition adds nothing in this regime.

## Open questions

1. Independent replication: all pupil/learning-rate evidence I opened is from Nassar's group. I did not open an independent-lab replication, nor the 2025 preprint (snippet only). Did anyone separate Omega from tau with a design that decorrelates them?
